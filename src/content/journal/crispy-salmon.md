Salmon used to be one of the things I made all the time. I still love it, but I feel like a lot of people overcook it or don’t really season it. It can be so much better.

## Start with the pieces

I lay the salmon horizontally on the cutting board and cut across it into pieces about two inches wide. Then I season with paprika, salt, pepper, cayenne, and sometimes cumin too.

The paprika gives it that orange-red color I love as it cooks. And yes, there needs to be enough salt.

## Skin side first

I pat it dry, then start skin side down in the pan so the skin gets really crispy. After that I flip it. I also like searing the sides, so it gets that crispness all around.

Also, turn on the vent fan. Seriously. It gets steamy in there lol.

## DO NOT OVERCOOK IT

I pay attention to the texture as it cooks, including a gentle press to feel how firm it’s getting. But feel alone doesn’t tell you whether it’s safely cooked. [Use a thermometer to check for 145°F in the thickest part](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/safe-temperature-chart).

I’m looking for cooked salmon that still has some life to it, not something that’s been forgotten in the pan. I don’t have one exact cooking time to give you because the pieces aren’t always the same thickness.

## What goes next to it

Avocado and vegetables are always good. So are dill rice, roasted potatoes, or a salad. The salmon with avocado salad pictured here is one of my favorites.

A crispy piece of salmon and something fresh next to it. That’s a very good plate to me.
