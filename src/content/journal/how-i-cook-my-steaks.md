I love steak, obviously. I’ve spent a lot of time getting better at different ways of cooking it, and I have opinions now.

If I’m cooking indoors, cast iron is my favorite. But the grill is really the best to me. I’ve grown to love it.

## Start with the thickness

The measurement I actually pay attention to here is how thick the steak is. That makes more sense to me than giving every steak the same amount of time.

For a 1.5-inch New York strip, around five minutes per side on the grill is my rough starting point. That isn’t a promise about when it’ll be done. Grill heat and the steak itself change things, so check the internal temperature rather than relying only on the clock.

For a food-safety reference, [USDA recommends 145°F in the center of a whole-cut steak, followed by at least a three-minute rest](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/safe-temperature-chart).

## Give the fat some attention

Especially with a New York strip, I like to render the fat edge first, then sear and flip. And I don’t press down on the steak while it cooks. I want to keep those juices in it.

## Rosemary, garlic, butter

Basting is one of my favorite parts when I’m cooking in a pan. Rosemary, garlic, and butter are the combination I love. If I want something a little lighter, I skip the butter baste.

These are my habits, not a full measured recipe. I’m still very much an eyeball-it kind of cook.

## The infamous steak frites

My steak frites with chimichurri is probably my most infamous dish among my friends and relatives. It’s one of the things I love making for the people I love, and it definitely deserves a place here.
