I wasn’t born with magical cooking powers. I just love cooking, and I keep trying things.

There was a point when I had no idea how to cook scallops or octopus. But I wanted to eat them, so I learned. That’s really it. You don’t have to already know how to do something to give it a try.

## Look up the part you don’t know

If I need help, I go online and look up how to cook the thing. Learning the technique doesn’t mean I have to copy every seasoning or make the whole dish exactly the same way.

Once I understand the basics, I can use the spices I like and make it feel like my food. I still check the practical things, like how to cook it safely. The experimenting is in finding the flavors and combinations I love.

## Have your little spice kit

It helps so much to have a few things you always keep around. For me, that’s paprika, salt, garlic powder, pepper, cayenne, cumin, and turmeric. I know I like them, so I have somewhere to start.

Mix and match. Try a different side. Make something you’re curious about. Not everything has to be a big production, and not everything has to be perfect the first time.

## You learn by doing it

The more I cook, the more I figure out what I like and what I want to do differently next time. That’s how I’ve gotten better, not by being afraid to mess something up.

If there’s something you keep wanting to make, look up what you need to know and try it. Loving what you’re doing goes a long way.
